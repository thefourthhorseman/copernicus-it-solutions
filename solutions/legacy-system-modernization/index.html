Sound familiar?

Signs your core systems are holding the business back.

  • Every new product, partner or market takes months longer than it should, because the core system has to change first.
  • A handful of people understand how the platform really works, and leadership knows what happens if they leave.
  • Incidents, slowdowns at peak or failed overnight batches have come to be treated as normal.
  • Infrastructure, databases or frameworks are approaching end of support, and the upgrade path is unclear.
  • Each new SaaS tool, customer integration or acquisition means another round of custom work.
  • Investors, buyers or the board ask questions about technology risk that are hard to answer with confidence.
  • Keeping the lights on consumes most of the IT budget, leaving little for anything new.
  • A rewrite has been discussed for years, but nobody can say what it would cost or how to do it safely.
Why the usual approach falls short

Most modernization efforts go wrong before the first line of code.

It is treated as an IT problem

A technical health check produces a list of defects. It does not tell the CEO, the board or investors what the risk means for revenue, valuation or the next three years of the plan.

The big-bang rewrite

Replacing everything at once concentrates risk, freezes the business for years and often runs out of budget before it delivers value.

Rebuilding the past

Most programs recreate today's system on newer technology. The chance to change what the platform can do for the business, and how it earns money, is missed.

Advice without accountability

Recommendations from people who will never have to deliver them tend to underestimate the hard parts: data migration, integration, cut-over and the people who run the system today.

Executive summary preview

The first page your board reads.

Recommendation: progressive modernization, not a rewrite.

The order platform is reliable today, but two risks threaten the growth plan within 18 months: dependence on two engineers, and the end of vendor support for its core stack.

  • Stabilize first: document the pricing engine, add a second team, upgrade the database. 3 months.
  • Then separate pricing and partner integration from the core, which cuts onboarding from 10 weeks to about 2.
  • Estimated investment $1.1–1.4M over 18 months, against $3M+ and 30 months for a full rewrite.

Decision needed this quarter: fund phase 1 and name an executive sponsor.
