Sound familiar?

Where the work gets stuck.

  • Critical processes run on spreadsheets and email threads that only a few people can follow.
  • Teams re-key the same data into several systems, and errors surface downstream.
  • Approvals wait for days in inboxes, and nobody can see where a request stands.
  • Your SaaS applications do not talk to each other, so people do the integration by hand.
  • Skilled staff spend hours every week on repetitive administrative tasks.
  • Answers depend on knowing who to ask, or on documents nobody can find.
  • Growth means hiring more people to do the same manual work.
  • You have tried AI tools, but the way the work actually gets done has not changed.
Why the usual approach falls short

Why most automation projects disappoint.

Automating the old process

Putting a bot or a language model on top of a broken process makes the broken process faster. The duplicate steps, the waiting and the rework stay.

Starting with the tool

Choosing a platform before understanding the work leads to proofs of concept that impress in a demo and never reach production.

AI where a rule would do

Many steps are better served by a business rule, an API call or a simple workflow: cheaper to run, faster, and easier to audit.

Governance as an afterthought

Without clear controls on data, privacy, auditability and human approval, pilots stall at the risk and compliance review, or create exposure nobody signed off on.

The Copernicus approach

Redesign the work first. Then choose the technology.

We map how the work really happens, remove what should not exist, and only then decide, step by step, what each part of the process needs. Often that is a rule or an integration. Sometimes it is an AI agent. Sometimes it should stay with a person.

  • Deterministic business rulesWhere the logic is known and must be exact.
  • Workflow automationRouting, approvals, notifications and hand-offs.
  • APIs and system integrationSo data is entered once and flows everywhere it is needed.
  • Data pipelinesReliable, timely data for reporting and decisions.
  • AI agents and LLM-based reasoningFor unstructured inputs, classification, drafting and multi-step tasks.
  • Enterprise knowledge retrieval (RAG)Answers grounded in your own documents and records.
  • Human-in-the-loop approvalPeople confirm what matters before it takes effect.
  • Human judgmentWhere risk, relationships or accountability require it.
  1. Understand the workWalkthroughs with the people who do the work, and the systems, documents and data it touches.
  2. Map and measureVolumes, cycle times, hand-offs, error rates, and where capacity is lost.
  3. RedesignSimplify, remove duplicate steps and rethink approvals before anything is automated.
  4. Match each step to the right approachRules, integration, workflow, AI or a person, chosen on value, risk and complexity.
  5. Prioritize and planA 90-day roadmap and one well-defined pilot that proves value quickly.
